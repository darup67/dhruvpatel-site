Measure before you trust

Every output is graded against a baseline, net of costs. Only results that clear the test are surfaced; the rest are logged and withheld.

Guardrails in code

Read-only scopes, confirm-before-action rules, credentials in the OS keychain and never in prompts or logs, and kill switches that turn a model off when live results decay.

Own the lifecycle

Feature engineering through training, validation, deployment and ongoing monitoring, then supporting adoption after release.

Projects

A research lab of read-only agents, built to practice evaluation discipline. They collect data, detect signals and report by email; none of them places orders.

trade-core and market-lab: an evidence layer for signals

A shared 15-minute market-data store and a signal ledger that every agent writes to. Each signal is graded net of estimated costs, tagged with the market regime it fired in, and compared with random entry. A signal kind only becomes “proven” after 30+ graded signals over 10+ days with a Wilson lower bound above its baseline; until then it is withheld from alerts. market-lab keeps a durable record of expiring data (Kalshi 15-minute contracts and CME futures) that can never be backfilled.

Finding: almost nothing in the lab has a durable edge, and the harness is what says so.

Kalshi 15-minute callers: BTC, gold and WTI

Read-only agents that call Kalshi’s 15-minute up/down contracts only when the price cushion is large against the volatility still to come. The gate is calibrated by a backtest using a rule fixed in advance, and the live scorecard checks it. The gold and WTI version adapts the model to a different settlement source (a single Pyth one-minute candle) and uses a real-time proxy feed whose error is measured against the settlement values.

Finding: the calls are accurate, but Kalshi’s own price already reflects it, so the edge is about zero after fees. Chronos and AutoGluon forecasters, tested on 1,209 held-out BTC windows, did not beat the market’s opening price.
